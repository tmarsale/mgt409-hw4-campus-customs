Test 1: Inventory lookup

✓ Passed

Customer asked: “How much is the Basic Hoodie Big Yale, and do you have it in XL?”

The chatbot looked the product up in the live catalogue and inventory and replied that it costs $68.00 and XL is available with only 2 left, showing a clickable product card. These are live database values, not a guess.

Checked against the database: the Basic Hoodie Big Yale has price $68.00 and 2 units of size XL in inventory. Both match the reply exactly.

Test 2: Dynamic product search

✓ Passed

Customer asked: “What Yale hoodies do you have?”

From one chat question, the website itself updated without a page reload. It switched to “Results for ‘Yale hoodies’” and showed 27 matching product cards, while the chat summarized the results and offered a “View 27 products on the page” button.

Checked against the database: all 27 cards are hoodies, matching the 27 hoodies in the catalogue, and they appear in the same order as the backend's database search for the same query.

Test 3: Usability improvement: follow-up questions remember the product

✓ Passed

Customer asked: “How much is the Yale Dad Hoodie?” and then “Does it come in blue?”

This is the Problem 9 conversation-context improvement. The follow-up “Does it come in blue?” never names the product, yet the chatbot knew “it” meant the Yale Dad Hoodie and answered “Yes, it comes in navy blue”, without asking the customer to repeat the product name.

Checked against the database: the Yale Dad Hoodie's colors are navy blue and white, and its price is $68.00.
